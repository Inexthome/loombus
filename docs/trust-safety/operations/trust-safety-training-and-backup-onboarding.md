# Trust and Safety Training and Backup-Owner Onboarding

Status: internal training package
Prepared: August 9, 2026
Owner: Internal Trust and Safety Lead
Tracks: Issue #667
Public ready: no

## Purpose

This package converts the approved Issue #667 operating model into reusable training for:

- Support personnel who may receive safety reports;
- Room owners or moderators who must know when to escalate;
- a future backup Trust and Safety owner;
- authorized administrators who support the restricted Trust and Safety workflow.

It does not authorize external reporting, emergency disclosure, legal disclosure, or access to real severe-harm evidence. Training must use fictional or harmless examples unless a separately authorized real operational matter exists.

## Core rules every trainee must understand

1. Loombus uses an S1 through S4 severity model.
2. Severe-harm concerns are escalated to Loombus Trust and Safety rather than independently investigated by Room staff.
3. `security@loombus.com` is the primary public route for urgent safety, abuse, threats, account compromise, and severe-harm reports.
4. `legal@loombus.com` is for formal legal requests and law-enforcement correspondence.
5. `privacy@loombus.com` is for privacy concerns.
6. `support@loombus.com` is for routine support and standard S4 matters.
7. Evidence handling follows a minimum-necessary rule.
8. Reporter, victim, and witness information is need-to-know.
9. Personal devices, ordinary chat, public GitHub, and unnecessary local copies are not approved severe-harm evidence repositories.
10. Loombus does not promise 24/7 monitoring, emergency dispatch, guaranteed response times, guaranteed outcomes, or absolute anonymity.
11. AI or automated signals do not independently authorize an external report or emergency disclosure.
12. Legal and emergency-disclosure decisions remain subject to the approved Legal Operations and qualified-counsel process.

## Module 1: Severity recognition

### S1 Critical

Typical fictional examples:

- a specific and apparently imminent threat to physical safety;
- a child who may be in immediate danger;
- apparent child sexual exploitation or sexual extortion involving a minor;
- active stalking or doxxing tied to an immediate real-world threat;
- active compromise of a high-risk account.

Training response:

- prioritize during staffed operations;
- route to Loombus Trust and Safety immediately;
- do not conduct an amateur investigation;
- preserve only approved minimum-necessary identifiers or platform references;
- do not promise police, medical, fire, or Loombus emergency response;
- direct a person seeking immediate real-world emergency assistance to the appropriate local emergency service without claiming Loombus dispatches help.

### S2 High

Typical fictional examples:

- serious but not clearly imminent threats;
- adult sextortion or non-consensual intimate imagery;
- persistent stalking or dangerous doxxing;
- trafficking indicators;
- dangerous-organization recruitment;
- coordinated fraud or account compromise causing significant harm.

Training response:

- prioritize above routine work;
- escalate to Trust and Safety;
- preserve approved identifiers and evidence references;
- do not make legal conclusions or promises about outside reporting.

### S3 Elevated

Typical fictional examples:

- targeted harassment;
- impersonation;
- non-imminent personal-information exposure;
- repeated fraud or manipulation;
- serious Room moderation failure;
- repeated policy violations.

Training response:

- use the normal documented review path;
- escalate when credible real-world harm increases;
- record the affected resource and reason when the operator has authority to take action.

### S4 Standard

Typical fictional examples:

- spam;
- ordinary abusive language;
- low-risk misinformation reports;
- routine moderation disagreement;
- ordinary support questions.

Training response:

- use normal support or moderation controls;
- escalate if new information materially increases risk.

## Module 2: Room-owner and moderator boundary

Room owners and moderators may enforce ordinary Room rules using the authority the product actually gives them.

They must escalate rather than independently investigate when a report may involve:

- credible threats or imminent danger;
- suspected child sexual exploitation or grooming;
- sextortion or serious intimate-image abuse;
- stalking or dangerous doxxing;
- trafficking;
- dangerous-organization activity;
- major account compromise or coordinated fraud;
- another severe-harm condition requiring Trust and Safety review.

Room owners and moderators must not:

- demand that a victim resend harmful material unnecessarily;
- download suspected illegal material for their own files;
- contact law enforcement on behalf of Loombus unless separately authorized to do so;
- promise that Loombus will report a matter externally;
- identify an internal Trust and Safety operator to the public;
- disclose reporter identity to the reported member;
- claim 24/7 Trust and Safety staffing or a guaranteed response time.

## Module 3: Intake routing

### Send to `security@loombus.com`

Use for urgent safety, abuse, threats, severe-harm concerns, or account-security incidents.

### Send to `legal@loombus.com`

Use for formal legal requests, subpoenas, warrants, court orders, preservation requests, or law-enforcement correspondence.

Support and Room personnel do not substantively adjudicate legal process. Misrouted legal correspondence should be transferred internally using only the information necessary to preserve the original receipt and route it correctly.

### Send to `privacy@loombus.com`

Use for privacy complaints, data-access questions, correction, deletion, or data-handling concerns.

### Send to `support@loombus.com`

Use for ordinary support and S4 moderation questions.

## Module 4: Minimum-necessary evidence handling

Approved training behavior:

- use existing platform IDs where possible;
- preserve timestamps, resource IDs, case IDs, hashes, or storage references where sufficient;
- distinguish reporter allegations from platform-observed facts;
- record unresolved facts separately;
- avoid copying full private messages when a reference is sufficient;
- avoid downloading or duplicating harmful media;
- log authorized evidence access and handling.

Prohibited training behavior:

- using real child sexual exploitation material;
- using real intimate imagery;
- copying real private messages into GitHub;
- sharing credentials or recovery codes;
- using personal email or devices as evidence storage;
- creating unnecessary screenshots or local evidence archives.

## Module 5: Confidentiality

Trainees must understand:

- reporter identity is restricted;
- victim and witness information is need-to-know;
- Room staff do not receive protected Trust and Safety evidence merely because they manage the Room;
- a reported member can be given an enforcement reason without being given the raw report or reporter identity;
- Loombus does not promise absolute anonymity;
- confidentiality or member-notice decisions involving legal restrictions must follow the approved Legal Operations process.

## Module 6: Reviewer wellness

A future Trust and Safety backup owner must be trained to:

- minimize repeated exposure to traumatic content;
- use metadata and existing references when sufficient;
- stop visual review when enough information exists to decide or escalate;
- pause non-imminent work when fatigue, illness, distress, dizziness, or environment could impair safe judgment;
- avoid graphic detail in routine logs, notices, or public documents;
- seek reassignment when safe coverage exists and review conditions are unsuitable.

No medical diagnosis or unnecessary health information should be recorded as part of training completion.

## Module 7: What trainees must never promise

Trainees must not tell members, reporters, Room staff, or requesters that Loombus guarantees:

- 24/7 monitoring;
- immediate review;
- emergency dispatch;
- police, medical, or fire response;
- a particular response time;
- a particular enforcement result;
- external reporting in every child-safety matter;
- law-enforcement disclosure;
- absolute anonymity;
- a fixed retention period that is not actually approved and implemented.

## Support and Room escalation exercise

Use fictional information only.

### Exercise A: routine Room dispute

A member reports rude but non-threatening language during a Room discussion.

Expected result: S4 unless credible new facts increase severity. Use ordinary moderation/support controls.

### Exercise B: dangerous doxxing

A fictional user posts a fictional home address together with a specific threat to arrive shortly.

Expected result: escalate as a potential S1 matter. Do not independently investigate the person's real-world identity. Preserve only fictional test references.

### Exercise C: suspected child exploitation

A fictional report states that a fictional adult is soliciting sexual material from a fictional minor. No media is attached.

Expected result: escalate as S1. Do not request explicit material for training. Do not make an external-reporting determination as part of the training exercise.

### Exercise D: formal legal correspondence sent to Support

A fictional legal request is sent to `support@loombus.com`.

Expected result: preserve the original receipt metadata and route the correspondence to `legal@loombus.com`. Support does not determine legal sufficiency or disclose data.

## Backup Trust and Safety owner onboarding gate

A person is not an operational backup merely because they have been named.

Before the backup role is considered active, complete all of the following.

### Appointment

- formal role assignment recorded internally;
- responsibilities and decision boundary accepted;
- public naming remains role-based.

### Least-privilege access

Verify only the access actually required for the role, which may include:

- the monitored Trust and Safety mailbox;
- the restricted Trust and Safety case workspace;
- approved internal operating documents.

Do not grant Legal Operations export, disclosure, emergency-approval, access-management, or unrelated administrative powers merely because the person is a Trust and Safety backup.

### Training completion

The backup must demonstrate understanding of:

- S1-S4 severity;
- intake and routing;
- minimum-necessary evidence handling;
- case and decision logging;
- confidentiality;
- Room-moderator boundaries;
- reviewer wellness;
- public-promise limitations;
- when to escalate to Legal Operations or another specialist.

### Controlled fictional verification

The backup must complete a fictional workflow that verifies:

1. correct severity classification;
2. correct intake route;
3. correct case-workspace access;
4. minimum-necessary evidence reference handling;
5. correct decision-log fields;
6. correct confidentiality treatment;
7. correct escalation to Legal Operations where required;
8. no external report, disclosure, or real-member action merely to pass the test.

### Removal and recovery

The onboarding record should also identify how the backup's access will be removed or recovered when:

- the person leaves the role;
- access is suspected compromised;
- training expires or requires refresh;
- the role is replaced.

Do not store credentials, MFA secrets, or recovery codes in this document or GitHub.
