# Teen Safety Overview

## Internal drafting notice

This document describes the proposed Loombus teen-safety system, not a fully implemented production contract. It must not be published until teen defaults, age-aware discovery and messaging rules, underage reporting, age correction, sensitive-content treatment, guardian information, Room duties, and legal operations are implemented and verified.

## Safety philosophy

Loombus should protect teens without making them feel trapped, constantly watched, or excluded from meaningful participation.

The design principles are:

- privacy by default rather than public exposure by default;
- limits on high-risk interactions rather than blanket surveillance;
- age-appropriate explanations rather than hidden restrictions;
- meaningful control over followers, messages, discoverability, and visibility;
- strong action against exploitation, grooming, threats, harassment, and doxxing;
- room for learning, debate, creativity, and age-appropriate independence;
- minimal collection of sensitive age and identity information;
- human review for high-impact decisions where practical.

## Age scope

Loombus is intended for people who meet the platform's minimum age. Teen protections should generally apply to members ages 13 through 17.

A birth date or age declaration does not prove age. Loombus must not claim universal age verification unless a verified age-assurance process is implemented.

The final policy must define:

- the minimum age;
- how age is collected and used;
- how a member corrects an incorrect age;
- how an underage account is reported;
- what happens when age cannot be confirmed;
- when parental or guardian involvement is legally required;
- what information is retained after age-related action.

## Proposed teen account defaults

The target design should consider the following defaults, subject to product and legal approval:

- Private account enabled by default;
- follower approval required;
- profile discoverability limited by default;
- future Discussions limited to an age-appropriate audience by default;
- viewer identity protections clearly explained;
- precise location, school, routine, and contact information discouraged;
- direct messages limited to appropriate existing relationships;
- adult-to-teen unsolicited contact restricted;
- sensitive-content recommendation reduced;
- public commercial or professional claims restricted where age makes participation unsuitable.

These are proposed requirements. They are not all verified in the current product and must not be presented as live until implemented.

## Teen control and understanding

Teen settings should explain:

- who can find the account;
- who can follow;
- who can message;
- who can view a Discussion;
- whether the teen appears in People and Search;
- how profile and Discussion viewers are recorded;
- how to block and report;
- what changes when the teen turns 18;
- which controls are fixed for safety and which may be changed.

Settings should use plain language and avoid manipulative prompts that pressure teens to become more public.

## Adult-to-teen interactions

High-risk contact can occur through messages, follows, Rooms, Events, Jobs, Services, Marketplace, Requests, or external links.

The target system should restrict or review:

- unsolicited private messages from adults without an established relationship;
- repeated follow requests after denial;
- requests for secrecy, private photos, school, location, schedule, or off-platform contact;
- gifts, money, job offers, status, or opportunities used to create dependency;
- sexualized comments or escalating boundary violations;
- adults seeking one-to-one contact through youth or Classroom Rooms;
- attempts to move a teen to a less protected channel.

General mutual-connection and blocking controls exist, but age-aware rules are not fully verified.

## Grooming and exploitation

Any sexual solicitation, grooming, sextortion, or sexualized depiction of a minor is prohibited. Reports should receive elevated review.

Teens should be told:

- they are not at fault for manipulation or coercion;
- they should not send more content or money to stop a threat;
- they should preserve the account or message location without redistributing harmful material;
- they can block and report;
- immediate danger should be directed to local emergency services or a trusted adult.

Loombus must not claim a specialized response time until child-safety operations are established.

## Harassment, bullying, and social pressure

Teen safety should address:

- group harassment and exclusion;
- rumor campaigns;
- identity-based abuse;
- appearance or body shaming;
- threats to expose messages or images;
- impersonation accounts;
- coordinated reporting;
- pressure to participate in dangerous challenges;
- harmful eating-disorder or self-harm communities.

Blocking should not be presented as the teen's only responsibility. Severe or repeated targeting requires platform review.

## Content and recommendation protections

The target system should avoid recommending to teens:

- sexualized adult content;
- graphic violence;
- self-harm promotion;
- dangerous challenges;
- grooming or exploitative accounts;
- regulated-goods transactions;
- manipulative financial schemes;
- age-inappropriate Jobs, Services, Events, or Marketplace activity.

Loombus must inventory the actual recommendation and Search systems before making strong claims. A Discussion being accessible does not mean it should be broadly recommended to teens.

## Location and personal information

Teens should be discouraged from publicly sharing:

- home address;
- live location;
- daily routine;
- school schedule;
- private phone or email;
- travel plans;
- government documents;
- financial information;
- private medical details;
- images that reveal a sensitive location.

Local Discovery should continue using approximate location. Room and Event leaders should minimize exposure of teen locations and schedules.

## Rooms involving teens

Room owners and moderators in Classroom, neighborhood, youth, organization, or community Rooms should:

- use controlled admission;
- limit directories and private contact information;
- assign staff roles carefully;
- prohibit private coercion and sexualized interaction;
- provide clear Room rules;
- keep reports confidential;
- escalate exploitation, credible threats, and serious safety concerns;
- avoid claiming that Loombus verified educators, guardians, schools, coaches, or volunteers.

The product should consider whether one-to-one adult and teen communication requires additional visibility or restrictions without exposing private conversations broadly.

## Jobs, Services, Marketplace, and commercial activity

Teen participation may require additional restrictions. The final policy should define:

- whether teens may sell or purchase Marketplace items;
- which goods are prohibited regardless of age;
- whether teens may post or respond to Jobs;
- age-appropriate work and application safety;
- restrictions on professional, financial, medical, or legal Services;
- payment, contract, and appointment limitations;
- how businesses and employers may contact teen members.

No commercial eligibility should be implied until verified in the product and reviewed legally.

## Parents and guardians

A parent or guardian resource should explain:

- what Loombus is;
- which teen controls exist;
- what Loombus can and cannot see or verify;
- how to report an underage account or safety concern;
- how privacy and data requests work;
- when Loombus may be unable to disclose account information;
- why healthy conversation with the teen is preferable to secret surveillance.

Loombus should not build guardian access that exposes private teen communications without a clear legal and safety basis.

## Data minimization

Age-related systems should collect the least information necessary. Loombus should avoid storing identity documents or biometric estimates unless a documented need, vendor review, privacy assessment, security design, retention limit, and legal basis exist.

Teen data should not be used to create exploitative advertising or engagement profiles. The final privacy policy must accurately describe actual data flows.

## Reporting and support

Teen reporting should be easy to understand and should identify high-risk categories such as:

- sexual contact or grooming;
- threats or stalking;
- intimate-image abuse;
- doxxing;
- self-harm encouragement;
- fraud or coercion;
- unsafe adult contact;
- underage-account concerns.

The system should avoid requiring teens to provide repeated traumatic detail.

## Enforcement

Severe exploitation, grooming, sextortion, threats, doxxing, or repeated adult boundary violations may justify immediate account and Room action.

A teen seeking help should not be punished solely for expressing distress. Enforcement should target harmful conduct, unsafe facilitation, or other violations.

## Turning 18

The target system should explain which teen defaults change, whether the member is notified, and whether privacy settings remain in place unless the member chooses otherwise. Becoming an adult should not automatically make an account maximally public.

## Publication blockers

Before publication, Loombus must:

1. confirm the minimum age and jurisdictional requirements;
2. implement and test teen privacy defaults;
3. implement age-aware messaging and discovery rules;
4. create underage reporting and age correction;
5. establish child-safety escalation and record handling;
6. define teen commerce eligibility;
7. define Room duties involving minors;
8. inventory recommendation and AI treatment for teens;
9. create parent and guardian information;
10. complete child-safety, privacy, accessibility, and legal review.
