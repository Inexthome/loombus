# Reporting Guide

## Internal drafting notice

This is an internal draft for the proposed member-facing reporting guide. It must be verified against every live report control, API, moderation queue, notification, Room workflow, support channel, and emergency process before publication.

## Purpose

Reporting gives members a way to ask Loombus or a Room's authorized staff to review content, conduct, listings, messages, or activity that may violate rules or create a safety concern.

A report is a request for review. It does not guarantee removal, account action, payment recovery, legal action, or a particular outcome.

## What members may report

Depending on the available controls, members may be able to report:

- a Discussion;
- a Reply;
- a person or profile;
- a private message;
- a Room Discussion, Reply, attachment, member, or Room activity;
- a Marketplace listing;
- a Business, Service, Request, Job, Event, or Appointment-related record;
- impersonation, fraud, intellectual-property concerns, or account compromise;
- a technical or accessibility issue through Support rather than a conduct report.

The final public guide must list only report types that are present and working in production.

## Choose the closest report target

Members should report the narrowest relevant item:

- report a **Discussion** when the opening content is the issue;
- report a **Reply** when a specific response is the issue;
- report a **person** when the concern involves a broader pattern, identity, impersonation, or conduct across multiple places;
- report a **message** when the harmful content was sent privately;
- report the **listing or record** when the concern is a Marketplace item, Job, Service, Request, Event, or Business claim;
- report to **Room staff** for ordinary Room-rule issues;
- report to **Loombus** when conduct may violate Loombus-wide safety standards or Room leadership is involved.

Submitting multiple duplicate reports does not increase priority and may slow review.

## Information that helps review

A useful report should state:

- what happened;
- which rule or harm appears relevant;
- who or what is affected;
- whether the behavior is repeated;
- whether there is a threat, minor, intimate material, doxxing, fraud, account compromise, or immediate danger;
- relevant dates, messages, transaction details, or Room context;
- whether a block, follow denial, Room removal, or request to stop was bypassed;
- whether supporting information already exists within Loombus.

Members should not include passwords, complete payment-card numbers, government identification numbers, unnecessary medical information, or copies of illegal sexual material.

## What not to do

Members should not:

- repost harmful content to draw attention to it;
- download or forward suspected child sexual exploitation material;
- publicly expose private information while trying to report doxxing;
- contact or confront a suspected scammer solely to gather evidence;
- organize mass reports or ask others to report content they have not reviewed;
- threaten the reported person;
- submit false or retaliatory reports;
- use reporting to settle ordinary personal, political, business, or Room disagreements.

Knowingly false, coordinated, or retaliatory reporting may itself violate Loombus rules.

## Reports involving immediate danger

Loombus is not an emergency service. When someone faces immediate physical danger, members should contact local emergency services.

A Loombus report may still help preserve and review platform activity, but Loombus must not promise real-time monitoring, emergency dispatch, or a guaranteed response time until an operationally verified emergency process exists.

## Reports involving minors

Suspected sexual exploitation, grooming, sextortion, or severe physical danger involving a minor should receive elevated review.

Members should not investigate privately, copy harmful material, or pressure the minor to provide public proof. The report should identify the account, content, or communication and explain the concern without unnecessary graphic detail.

Loombus must establish and legally review its external child-safety reporting obligations before publishing detailed promises.

## Reports involving intimate imagery

The depicted person, an authorized representative, or someone with credible knowledge should be able to report non-consensual intimate imagery or threats to distribute it.

The reporting route should minimize repeated exposure and should not require the affected person to repost the material. Identity verification, where needed, should be secure and proportionate.

## Fraud and transaction reports

Members should report the relevant listing, business, service, request, job, event, appointment, message, or account. Helpful information may include:

- the representation that appears false;
- the amount or item involved;
- the payment method or external destination, without posting full credentials;
- whether goods, services, employment, or access were promised;
- whether another account or business is being impersonated;
- whether the matter also involves threats or account compromise.

Loombus should not promise refunds, chargebacks, escrow, professional verification, or dispute resolution unless those services are actually provided.

## Room reporting paths

Room owners and moderators may review Room-level reports under the Room's rules. Loombus-wide reports remain appropriate when:

- the issue involves threats, exploitation, hate, doxxing, fraud, or other severe harm;
- Room staff are involved;
- the Room's rules conflict with Loombus-wide standards;
- a member reasonably fears retaliation;
- the conduct extends beyond the Room.

Room moderation resolution may record an outcome without automatically removing content or changing membership. The guide must explain the difference between resolving a case and carrying out a separate enforcement action.

## After a report is submitted

A mature reporting system should:

1. confirm receipt;
2. record the report category and target;
3. protect the reporter's identity where possible;
4. route the report by severity and module;
5. preserve necessary evidence;
6. prevent unauthorized access;
7. show a status where the product supports it;
8. notify the reporter of a meaningful outcome where appropriate;
9. provide appeal or additional-review information when available.

This full cross-platform experience is not yet verified. Public language must reflect actual functionality rather than the target state.

## Confidentiality and privacy

Loombus should not disclose a reporter's identity to the reported person unless disclosure is necessary, lawful, and approved. A reported person may receive the policy reason and affected content without receiving confidential reporter details.

Reports, evidence, and reviewer notes should be retained only under an approved schedule and accessed by personnel with a legitimate need.

## Possible outcomes

A report may result in:

- no violation found;
- education or a warning;
- a content label or distribution limit;
- content or attachment removal;
- a feature restriction;
- account or Room action;
- commercial-record action;
- specialist, legal, safety, or security escalation;
- a request for more information;
- closure because the matter is outside Loombus's role.

Loombus should avoid telling reporters that private personnel action occurred where disclosure would violate privacy or safety.

## Disagreement with an outcome

A reporter may be able to provide additional information or request review where the product offers that option. The person affected by enforcement may have a separate appeal path.

A universal report-outcome appeal system is not yet verified. The public guide must not promise one until implemented.

## Blocking and self-protection

Reporting and blocking serve different purposes. Reporting asks for review. Blocking limits supported interactions and visibility.

A block may not erase historical references, screenshots, legal records, authorized administrator access, or content visible through unrelated accounts. Members should use both controls when appropriate and review their privacy and discoverability settings.

## Publication blockers

Before publication, Loombus must:

1. inventory every live report control and report target;
2. standardize categories and severity routing;
3. assign module and escalation owners;
4. define reporter notices and outcome language;
5. confirm Room-versus-Loombus reporting boundaries;
6. establish child-safety, emergency, intimate-image, fraud, and security procedures;
7. define report and evidence retention;
8. verify reporter confidentiality and access controls;
9. align appeal language with the implemented system;
10. complete legal and accessibility review.
