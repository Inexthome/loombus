# Suicide and Self-Harm

## Internal drafting notice

This is an internal safety draft. It must not be published until Loombus confirms crisis-resource presentation, escalation ownership, reviewer training, regional limitations, teen handling, privacy safeguards, and the boundaries of any automated detection.

## Why this standard exists

People should be able to talk honestly about suicidal thoughts, self-harm, eating disorders, grief, recovery, and mental-health experiences. Those conversations can be important sources of support and understanding. At the same time, content that encourages, instructs, pressures, or sensationalizes self-harm can increase risk.

This standard is designed to permit help-seeking and recovery-oriented discussion while restricting content that materially promotes or facilitates harm.

## Content that is generally allowed

Loombus generally allows:

- a person expressing distress or asking for help;
- first-person discussion of past or present suicidal thoughts or self-harm;
- recovery stories and coping strategies;
- supportive responses that encourage safety and professional help;
- educational, medical, scientific, journalistic, artistic, or policy discussion;
- memorial content that does not romanticize or encourage imitation;
- discussion of eating-disorder recovery and body-image harm;
- criticism of unsafe treatment, institutions, or public policy;
- reporting content that appears to place someone at risk.

Allowed content may still receive a supportive prompt, resource notice, reduced recommendation, or sensitive-content treatment where the product supports it.

## Prohibited content

Loombus prohibits content that:

- encourages a person to die by suicide or seriously harm themselves;
- pressures, dares, shames, or coerces someone into self-harm;
- provides instructions intended to make suicide or serious self-harm easier;
- organizes pacts, challenges, competitions, or coordinated acts of self-harm;
- sells, promotes, or directs people to tools or services for the purpose of self-harm;
- glorifies or romanticizes suicide or self-harm in a way likely to encourage imitation;
- presents severe self-harm as desirable, cleansing, admirable, or necessary;
- targets a vulnerable person with messages designed to worsen risk;
- shares graphic self-harm media primarily to shock, celebrate, instruct, or harass;
- promotes eating-disorder behaviors, extreme restriction, purging, or dangerous weight-control practices as goals to pursue;
- uses private messages, Rooms, or external links to recruit people into harmful communities.

## High-risk expressions and imminent danger

A statement of intent, a specific plan, access to means, a stated time or place, a farewell message, or other credible indicators may require elevated review.

Loombus must not promise continuous monitoring, clinical assessment, emergency dispatch, or a guaranteed response time. Members should contact local emergency services when someone faces immediate danger. Loombus is not a crisis service or medical provider.

Before publication, Loombus must decide when and how it may present crisis resources, contact a member, preserve records, or make a lawful emergency disclosure. Those procedures require legal, privacy, and operational approval.

## Supportive responses

Members responding to distress should be encouraged to:

- take the person seriously;
- communicate care without judgment;
- encourage contact with trusted people and qualified support;
- avoid promising secrecy where immediate danger may exist;
- avoid debating whether the person's pain is legitimate;
- avoid sharing graphic details or methods;
- use reporting tools when a credible risk is present.

Members must not impersonate licensed professionals or make misleading clinical claims.

## Graphic and descriptive material

Graphic depictions or highly detailed descriptions may be removed or restricted when they increase risk, instruct, sensationalize, or target a person.

Context may support retaining limited material for medical, educational, documentary, journalistic, artistic, or prevention purposes. Loombus should use the least harmful presentation available, such as warnings, reduced previews, limited recommendation, or removal of unnecessary detail, but only where those controls are technically supported.

## Eating disorders and harmful body-image content

Loombus should distinguish recovery and awareness content from promotion. Prohibited conduct includes:

- encouraging dangerous restriction, purging, or compulsive behavior;
- sharing competitive targets or instructions intended to worsen an eating disorder;
- shaming or coercing a person into dangerous weight-control behavior;
- selling or promoting products through false or dangerous claims tied to disordered eating;
- directing vulnerable members to communities that normalize serious harm.

General fitness, nutrition, medical, and body-image discussion may be allowed, subject to the Services and Professional Claims Standard and applicable commerce rules.

## Teens

Teen members may face elevated risk from social pressure, bullying, sexual exploitation, and harmful recommendation loops. The Teen Safety overview should address:

- age-appropriate resource language;
- privacy and discoverability defaults;
- limits on adult-to-teen contact;
- sensitive-content recommendation treatment;
- parent or guardian information where legally appropriate;
- Room owner duties when minors participate.

These systems are not fully verified. Public language must not claim protections that are not implemented.

## Reporting and privacy

Reports should focus on the content and the apparent risk. Reporters should not publicly investigate, demand proof, or expose the person.

Internal review should:

- limit access to necessary personnel;
- avoid copying sensitive material unnecessarily;
- separate safety notes from public content;
- record the basis for escalation;
- disclose information only under approved legal and safety procedures;
- avoid using a report as evidence of wrongdoing by the person in distress.

Expressions of distress should not be treated as misconduct merely because they are difficult or urgent.

## Possible platform response

Depending on risk and context, Loombus may:

- provide a supportive notice or resource prompt;
- reduce recommendation or graphic exposure;
- remove content that encourages or facilitates harm;
- restrict accounts that target, coerce, or recruit vulnerable people;
- suspend or permanently remove accounts involved in severe encouragement or organized harm;
- preserve records or make lawful disclosures under approved procedures.

Loombus should avoid punitive enforcement against a person solely for seeking help. Enforcement should target harmful facilitation, coercion, abuse, or repeated policy violations.

## Appeals

Contextual appeals may be important for medical, documentary, artistic, educational, and recovery content. The general appeals system is not yet verified, so public language must describe only available review paths.

## Publication blockers

Before publication, Loombus must:

1. define crisis-resource and regional presentation rules;
2. assign safety escalation owners and reviewer training;
3. define imminent-risk assessment and documentation procedures;
4. approve emergency disclosure and preservation rules;
5. establish teen-specific treatment;
6. verify automated detection claims and limitations;
7. create non-punitive help-seeking guidance;
8. complete medical, privacy, and legal review.
