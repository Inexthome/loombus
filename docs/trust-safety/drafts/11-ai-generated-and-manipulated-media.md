# AI-Generated and Manipulated Media

## Internal drafting notice

This is an internal draft. It requires AI product, Trust and Safety, Privacy, Security, Legal, Search, and moderation review before publication. The final standard must match the disclosure controls and enforcement capabilities actually available in Loombus.

## Why this standard exists

AI and editing tools can support creativity, accessibility, education, analysis, and communication. They can also fabricate evidence, impersonate people, create sexual or abusive depictions, distort civic information, and scale deception.

Loombus does not prohibit content merely because AI helped create it. The central questions are whether the content deceives people about a material fact, harms an identifiable person, facilitates prohibited conduct, or is presented without necessary context.

## Definitions

For this standard:

- **AI-generated content** is text, audio, image, video, or other media substantially produced by a generative system.
- **Manipulated media** is authentic material that has been materially changed, combined, reordered, relabeled, or presented out of context.
- **Synthetic identity content** depicts or imitates an identifiable person, organization, voice, likeness, credential, or event.
- **Material disclosure** is a clear indication that reasonable viewers are likely to notice before relying on the content.

Minor editing, color correction, cropping, noise removal, spelling assistance, and ordinary formatting generally do not require disclosure unless they materially change meaning.

## Prohibited uses

Members may not use AI or manipulation to:

- impersonate a person or organization for fraud, harassment, sexual exploitation, or material deception;
- fabricate evidence of a crime, statement, endorsement, professional qualification, transaction, event, injury, or public action;
- create non-consensual intimate imagery or sexualized depictions of an identifiable person;
- create sexualized material involving a real or apparent minor;
- generate threats, extortion, doxxing, targeted abuse, or hateful propaganda;
- mislead people about election procedures, emergency instructions, public-health directions, or other high-risk civic facts;
- create fake job offers, business records, service credentials, listings, invoices, reviews, testimonials, or support communications;
- conceal the artificial origin of content where that origin would materially affect a reasonable person's decision;
- create or operate deceptive account networks or fake engagement;
- evade content, identity, duplicate, copyright, safety, or account enforcement systems.

## Disclosure expectations

Material disclosure should be required when synthetic or manipulated content could reasonably cause people to believe that:

- an identifiable person said or did something they did not say or do;
- a real event occurred differently from how it occurred;
- a product, service, job, business, organization, or public authority made a statement or endorsement;
- media is authentic evidence rather than a reconstruction, simulation, parody, or illustration;
- a testimonial, review, professional credential, or transaction is genuine.

Disclosure should be clear, close to the content, and not hidden only in tags or a distant profile description.

Loombus does not yet have a verified platform-wide AI-labeling system. Public policy must not promise automated labels, provenance detection, or reliable detection of all synthetic content until those systems exist.

## Allowed uses

Loombus generally allows:

- clearly disclosed creative, fictional, artistic, educational, or satirical content;
- AI-assisted writing and editing;
- accessibility uses such as translation, transcription, captioning, and voice assistance;
- simulations and reconstructions that are accurately labeled;
- commentary or criticism using altered media where a reasonable viewer can understand the context;
- anonymized or fictional personas that do not impersonate a real person or mislead people about material authority;
- research and discussion of AI systems, including examples used responsibly;
- AI-generated illustrations for Discussions, Businesses, Services, Events, or other records when they do not misrepresent the real offering.

Other standards still apply, including intellectual property, harassment, hate, privacy, fraud, and sexual-content rules.

## Public figures and matters of public concern

Satire, parody, commentary, and political expression involving public figures may receive broader protection. Content may still be prohibited or labeled when it creates a material deception about voting, emergencies, violence, criminal evidence, financial conduct, or another high-risk fact.

The presence of a public figure does not excuse non-consensual intimate content, threats, doxxing, fraud, or exploitation.

## Evidence and verification

Members should not present synthetic media as verified evidence. When a Discussion depends on disputed media, members should be encouraged to:

- identify the source;
- distinguish original material from edited copies;
- disclose reconstruction or generation;
- provide corroborating sources;
- avoid overstating certainty.

Loombus may use contextual notices, reduced recommendation, or removal where supported by the product. The policy must not claim forensic verification or reliable AI detection unless a tested system exists.

## Commercial and professional use

Businesses, employers, sellers, providers, and organizers must not use AI to create materially false representations of:

- products or inventory;
- locations, facilities, staff, customers, or completed work;
- credentials, licenses, certifications, testimonials, or reviews;
- compensation, duties, job conditions, availability, or application status;
- service outcomes, medical results, legal results, or financial returns;
- event attendance, speakers, venues, or sponsorships.

Illustrative media should be labeled when it could be mistaken for the actual offering.

## Relationship to Loombus AI

Loombus may provide AI-assisted drafting, summaries, maps, recommendations, or Search features. Those outputs may be incomplete, inaccurate, or biased. Use of a Loombus AI feature does not make a member's content verified or approved.

The separate AI and Automated Systems Notice should explain provider data flows, private-content treatment, retention, human review, and contesting automated outcomes. Those details are not yet fully inventoried.

## Reporting and review

Reports should identify:

- what appears generated or manipulated;
- the person, organization, or event being represented;
- the material false impression;
- whether fraud, sexual content, threats, civic harm, or a minor is involved;
- any known original source.

Reviewers should assess context and impact rather than relying only on detection tools. Detection results should be treated as signals, not conclusive proof.

## Possible enforcement

Loombus may:

- request or apply disclosure where supported;
- label, limit recommendation, or remove content;
- disable attachments or links;
- restrict messages, listings, Rooms, or account features;
- suspend or permanently remove accounts for severe or repeated deceptive use;
- preserve records for safety, fraud, legal, or appeal review.

## Appeals

Appeals may be appropriate for satire, parody, art, authorized voice or likeness use, documentary reconstruction, accessibility, mistaken identity, or content that was adequately disclosed. The platform-wide appeals system is not yet verified.

## Publication blockers

Before publication, Loombus must:

1. decide which content types require disclosure;
2. build or confirm member disclosure controls;
3. define civic, emergency, sexual, and fraud escalation;
4. inventory AI detection and provenance capabilities;
5. align commercial modules and Loombus AI features;
6. define reviewer standards for uncertain detection;
7. establish notice and appeal procedures;
8. complete AI, privacy, intellectual-property, and legal review.
