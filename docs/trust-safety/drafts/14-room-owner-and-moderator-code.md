# Room Owner and Moderator Code

## Internal drafting notice

This is an internal governance draft. It must be verified against Room roles, permissions, invitations, join requests, moderation, evidence, notifications, retention, billing, ownership transfer, archive, deletion, Customer Support isolation, organization controls, and audit history before publication.

## Purpose

Rooms allow communities, businesses, HOAs, classrooms, support teams, organizations, and private groups to operate with their own membership and workflows. Room owners and moderators receive authority over access, rules, content, roles, and operations. That authority carries duties.

This code establishes the minimum Loombus-wide responsibilities for Room leadership. A Room may adopt stricter rules, but it may not authorize conduct prohibited by Loombus or remove a member's right to report serious harm to Loombus.

## Roles and authority

The final public guide must match the exact production role model. In general:

- the **owner** holds primary responsibility for Room purpose, billing, ownership, governance, and high-risk lifecycle actions;
- **administrators** may manage broad Room operations delegated by the owner;
- **moderators** may review reports and manage conduct within granted permissions;
- other staff roles may operate specific modules or workflows;
- ordinary members retain the rights and responsibilities described by Room rules and Loombus-wide policies.

A title does not grant unlimited access. Server and database permissions remain authoritative.

## Core leadership duties

Room owners and moderators must:

- publish a clear Room purpose and rules;
- apply rules consistently and in good faith;
- distinguish disagreement from abuse;
- protect private member and report information;
- use the least authority necessary for the task;
- respond to credible safety concerns;
- keep Room-level enforcement separate from personal retaliation;
- respect Loombus-wide privacy, safety, billing, and legal boundaries;
- maintain accurate ownership and staff roles;
- cooperate with lawful Loombus safety and integrity review.

## Rule transparency

Room rules should explain:

- who may join;
- how admission works;
- what content and conduct are allowed;
- whether member posting or replies are limited;
- which roles may moderate;
- what actions Room staff may take;
- whether a Room-level review or appeal is available;
- how changes will be communicated;
- how Loombus-wide reports can be submitted.

Material rule changes should be communicated before enforcement where practical. Emergency safety changes may take effect immediately.

## Admission and membership

Room leadership must use invitations and join requests for legitimate Room purposes. They may not:

- use admission to facilitate unlawful discrimination;
- trick people into joining a Room under a false identity or purpose;
- repeatedly invite people who declined or blocked contact;
- sell or transfer invitations in unauthorized ways;
- bypass member limits or subscription controls;
- expose applicant information unnecessarily;
- admit suspended, removed, or ineligible people through alternate accounts to evade enforcement.

Free Rooms and paid Rooms may have different capacities and modules. Plan level does not alter safety obligations.

## Privacy and confidentiality

Room privacy limits access but does not guarantee secrecy. Members may take screenshots, recipients may copy content, and Loombus may access information as permitted for service operation, safety, security, legal compliance, and support.

Room leaders must:

- restrict sensitive tools to authorized roles;
- avoid exporting or republishing member directories without authorization;
- protect support cases, reports, evidence, join requests, forms, files, and internal notes;
- avoid posting private contact, medical, financial, employment, school, or household information without a legitimate need;
- use Customer Support case isolation as designed and not expose one customer's case to unrelated members;
- avoid promising confidentiality beyond the actual product and law.

## Moderation standards

Room moderators should:

- review the specific rule and evidence;
- consider context, history, severity, and member safety;
- avoid conflicts of interest where practical;
- document the outcome using approved fields;
- separate public explanations from internal notes;
- avoid reproducing harmful content unnecessarily;
- escalate child safety, credible threats, intimate-image abuse, doxxing, trafficking, or serious fraud to Loombus;
- carry out any separate content or membership action needed after resolving a report.

The canonical Room moderation workflow records reports, evidence snapshots, assignment, escalation, internal notes, responses, and outcomes. Resolving a report does not automatically remove content or change membership. Public guidance must state this clearly.

## Prohibited leadership conduct

Room owners and moderators may not:

- organize harassment, hate, threats, fraud, exploitation, or illegal activity;
- retaliate against a member for a good-faith safety report;
- reveal confidential reporter or victim information;
- fabricate evidence or moderation reasons;
- manipulate roles, suspensions, removals, or reports for personal, romantic, political, financial, or competitive retaliation;
- demand passwords, authentication codes, private payment credentials, or unnecessary identity documents;
- represent that Loombus verified credentials, licensing, guardianship, school authority, HOA authority, or employment status unless a verified program exists;
- use Room tools to evade platform-wide restrictions;
- interfere with a lawful investigation or legal hold;
- mislead members about billing, deletion, retention, or ownership consequences.

## Minors in Rooms

Classroom, neighborhood, youth, family, community, and organization Rooms may include minors. Leaders must use heightened care around:

- admission and directory visibility;
- adult-to-minor private communication;
- school, home, schedule, location, and guardian information;
- sexualized content or grooming behavior;
- images and Video Context involving minors;
- emergency and child-safety escalation;
- staff and volunteer access.

Loombus does not currently verify every educator, guardian, coach, school, or youth organization. Room leaders must not claim otherwise.

## Business, HOA, classroom, and support boundaries

### Business and workplace Rooms

Leaders must not use Room tools for unlawful discrimination, wage theft, retaliation, deceptive recruiting, or concealment of workplace hazards. Employment and records obligations require legal review outside Loombus.

### HOA and residential Rooms

Leaders should minimize residence and contact data, publish governance authority accurately, and avoid using Loombus as a substitute for legally required notices or records unless verified.

### Classroom Rooms

Leaders must use age-appropriate privacy, role, and communication practices. Loombus is not a student-information system or guardian-consent service unless separately implemented.

### Customer Support Rooms

Customer cases are designed for author-and-staff isolation with explicit participants. Staff must not widen access, reuse case information for unrelated purposes, or expose private attachments.

## Ownership and role administration

Ownership transfer is a high-risk action. Leaders must:

- transfer only to an eligible active member;
- understand the effect on billing, data, and authority;
- avoid coercive or fraudulent transfers;
- cancel pending transfers when necessary;
- maintain at least one responsible owner;
- use the audit history for material governance actions.

Organization administrators must not use cross-Room authority outside the organization's legitimate scope.

## Retention, archive, and deletion

Room leaders must not promise immediate deletion. Room lifecycle actions may involve:

- archive and recovery periods;
- active subscription checks;
- legal and retention holds;
- organization retention requirements;
- staged manifests and Storage deletion;
- retries and reconciliation;
- preserved audit or billing records.

Permanent deletion must remain subject to the production feature flag and state machine. Leaders may not use deletion to destroy evidence, evade a report, or violate a legal hold.

## Billing and plan representation

Owners must accurately represent Room capacity and features. They may not resell subscriptions or claim Enterprise commitments not contained in a signed agreement.

The current plan ladder distinguishes Single-Room and Organization plans. Organization member limits are per Room. Downgrades may be blocked when current membership or storage exceeds the target plan.

## Member notices and Room-level review

Where practical, Room staff should tell a member:

- what action was taken;
- which Room rule applies;
- whether the action is temporary or permanent;
- whether Room-level review is available;
- how to report a Loombus-wide safety issue.

Loombus should not promise to review every ordinary Room dispute. It should intervene where Loombus-wide standards, platform integrity, legal obligations, or severe safety concerns are implicated.

## Loombus action against Rooms

Loombus may restrict, suspend, or close a Room when:

- its purpose or sustained operation violates Loombus-wide standards;
- leaders organize or knowingly permit severe harm;
- leaders obstruct safety review or legal requirements;
- the Room is used for fraud, exploitation, dangerous organizations, or illegal transactions;
- governance or billing abuse creates material platform risk.

Eligible Room actions should support notice and review once the platform-wide appeals system is implemented.

## Publication blockers

Before publication, Loombus must:

1. verify the complete role and permission matrix;
2. define Room-level versus Loombus-wide report and appeal boundaries;
3. create a consistent moderator notice and reason-code standard;
4. confirm minors-in-Rooms operations;
5. verify support-case isolation and evidence access;
6. document retention, archive, deletion, and legal-hold behavior;
7. align Organization and Enterprise governance;
8. define intervention and leadership-removal procedures;
9. complete legal, privacy, safety, product, and accessibility review.
