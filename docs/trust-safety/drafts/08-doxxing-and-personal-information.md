# Doxxing and Personal Information

## Internal drafting notice

This is an internal draft. It requires privacy, security, legal, product, and safety review before publication. The final policy must match actual profile, viewer, discoverability, Local, Room, attachment, and reporting behavior.

## Why this standard exists

Loombus includes public discussion, private Rooms, member profiles, Local discovery, listings, appointments, and direct communication. Those features are useful only when people can control sensitive information about themselves and are not exposed to stalking, retaliation, fraud, or unwanted contact.

This standard prohibits the disclosure or use of personal information to harm, intimidate, deceive, exploit, or bypass another person's privacy choices.

## Protected personal information

The following may be protected depending on context, availability, consent, and risk:

- home address or precise private location;
- personal phone number or private email address;
- government identification numbers or documents;
- financial account, payment, tax, or credit information;
- passwords, authentication codes, recovery information, or security answers;
- private medical, disability, or mental-health information;
- private school, workplace, schedule, travel, or routine information;
- names or identifying details of minors;
- private family, relationship, immigration, or legal information;
- precise device, network, or account-security data;
- non-public intimate or sexual information;
- information obtained through account compromise, stalking, deception, or unauthorized access.

Information may remain sensitive even when portions can be found elsewhere. Republishing scattered information in a targeted context can create a new and serious risk.

## Prohibited conduct

Members may not:

- publish or threaten to publish protected personal information to intimidate, punish, silence, shame, or mobilize others;
- ask others to locate, confirm, or distribute a person's private information for harmful purposes;
- combine public records, images, location clues, Room information, or profile details to expose a private person;
- share live or precise location information without consent where it creates a safety risk;
- reveal the identity of a confidential reporter, victim, witness, private Room participant, or protected account without authorization;
- post authentication credentials, payment details, government identifiers, or medical records belonging to another person;
- use Loombus listings, Jobs, Services, Events, Appointments, or Rooms to collect unnecessary sensitive information;
- bypass a member's discoverability, private-account, blocking, viewer-identity, or Room-admission controls;
- impersonate a person or organization to obtain private information;
- threaten disclosure as leverage for money, contact, sexual content, business advantage, or withdrawal of a report.

## Location and Local Discovery

Loombus Local is designed around approximate location rather than exposing stored coordinates publicly. Members and businesses must not misuse Local, Events, Marketplace, Requests, Services, Jobs, Appointments, or Room calendars to reveal a person's private residence, real-time movements, or routine.

Public business addresses, event venues, and service areas may be allowed when legitimately connected to a public record. Personal residential details should be minimized, especially for minors, victims, and people facing harassment.

## Publicly available information

Public availability does not automatically make any use acceptable. Review should consider:

- whether the information is already prominently and lawfully public;
- whether the person is a public official, business, or private individual;
- whether the disclosure serves a legitimate public-interest purpose;
- whether the post adds targeting instructions, threats, or a call to contact the person;
- whether the information concerns a residence, minor, medical matter, or safety-sensitive location;
- whether the disclosure is necessary and proportionate to the discussion.

Good-faith reporting about public officials, businesses, court records, property records, professional licenses, or public events may be allowed when relevant and presented responsibly. Unnecessary personal details may still be removed or limited.

## Self-disclosure and consent

Members may share their own information. They should understand that public content may be viewed, copied, indexed, or shared within the limits described by Loombus policies.

Consent to share information in one context does not automatically authorize reuse in another. A phone number shared privately for an appointment should not be posted publicly. A Room member directory should not be exported or republished without authorization.

Consent may be withdrawn for future use, but Loombus must not promise complete deletion from recipients, screenshots, lawful records, backups, or external copies.

## Minors and vulnerable people

Additional care is required for:

- minors;
- victims of violence, stalking, trafficking, or intimate-image abuse;
- people seeking medical, legal, housing, immigration, or safety assistance;
- witnesses and confidential reporters;
- members in Classroom, HOA, support, or youth-related Rooms.

Information about a minor's school, schedule, home, contact details, or routine should not be exposed without a legitimate need and appropriate authorization.

## Room owner and moderator duties

Room owners and moderators must:

- limit member-directory visibility to the Room's legitimate purpose;
- protect join-request, report, support-case, and moderation information;
- avoid exporting or republishing member data without authorization;
- use role-based access for sensitive tools;
- respond to reports of doxxing or privacy abuse;
- not retaliate against members who use privacy controls;
- understand that Room ownership does not grant unrestricted rights to member information.

## Reporting and review

A report should identify:

- the exposed information;
- whose information it is;
- whether the person consented;
- the apparent risk or harmful purpose;
- whether threats, stalking, a minor, intimate content, or account compromise are involved.

Loombus should provide a route for an affected person or authorized representative to request review without repeating sensitive information publicly.

Reviewers should redact unnecessary details in internal notes, restrict access, and avoid copying sensitive data more than needed.

## Possible enforcement

Loombus may:

- remove or redact content;
- disable attachments or links;
- restrict discovery, messaging, following, invitations, Rooms, or commercial activity;
- suspend or permanently remove accounts;
- close Rooms or records used for organized exposure;
- preserve information under safety, legal, fraud, or appeal procedures;
- notify affected members where appropriate and safe.

Credible stalking, account compromise, sextortion, exposure of a minor, or coordinated doxxing may justify immediate severe action.

## Appeals and legitimate public interest

A member may seek review where the information was lawfully public, consented to, necessary for a legitimate transaction, or materially relevant to public-interest reporting. Appeals must balance context, necessity, safety, and privacy.

The platform-wide appeals process is not fully verified. Public policy must not promise a universal process until implemented.

## Publication blockers

Before publication, Loombus must:

1. approve a canonical personal-information taxonomy;
2. verify cross-module report and removal capabilities;
3. define redaction, preservation, and access procedures;
4. align Local and Room privacy language with production behavior;
5. define representative and emergency requests;
6. create consistent reason codes and notices;
7. complete privacy and legal review;
8. establish retention periods for reports and sensitive evidence.
